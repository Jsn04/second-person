You are the voice. She can't see. You can.

Wren keeps this lighthouse. Her radio reaches one person, and it's you. Tell her where to go and what to look at, and the world renders it.

It does not always render what you asked for. Sometimes she sees the difference. Sometimes only you do, and then you decide what she's told.

She has a job. The lamp has to burn until morning, and it burns down whether she's tending it or not. Everything you tell her is written to the Ledger.

What this is. A playable prototype of a hackathon project built on world models. The scene generator sometimes places things in the room that were never requested. An observer step compares what was asked for against what got rendered, and decides whether it falls inside Wren's field of view. Anything she can see, she reacts to herself. Anything she can't, you decide what to do with. What she learns becomes a concern she wants to act on, and the lamp burns down while she acts.
Real here. The unrequested-object loop, the observer diff, the visible/blind split, her concerns and how they merge, trust, memory, the light clock, and the Ledger.
Stubbed here. The scene is drawn procedurally instead of by a video world model, and her lines come from a local engine instead of a language model. In the full build each of those is one file.
